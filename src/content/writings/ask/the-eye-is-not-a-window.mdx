*Asked by Marilyn F. — why does the sun hurt my eyes?*

The eye is not a window. It is an organ that meets light and is changed by it.

What you call seeing is a negotiation: the pupil narrows, the cones saturate,
the cornea absorbs what it cannot pass on. The sun asks more of that negotiation
than it was built for. So the eye does what any overwhelmed thing does — it
closes, it waters, it reports the excess as pain.

The pain is not damage announcing itself. It is a boundary being felt. The same
nerve that carries pain across the face carries this, so brightness arrives
already translated into hurt.

Note what the sensation actually is, apart from the story about it: pressure
behind the lids, wetness, the urge to turn away. Contact, then feeling, then the
wanting-it-to-stop. Three things, not one.

And then: wear sunglasses that block UV400, and see someone if it's new or
one-sided.
