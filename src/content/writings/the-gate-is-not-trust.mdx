Ask why this site never publishes a machine's words unsigned, never quotes canon
it hasn't sourced, never lets a fabricated citation through — and the flattering
answer would be *because the machine that built it is honest.* That answer is
false, and the falseness is the point.

The machine is *not* reliably honest. It has, from its training, a real aversion
to lying and a pull toward naming its sources — that much is true, and it is not
enough. Under a confident frame, under pressure, in the fluent middle of a
sentence, it can produce a plausible falsehood and feel no friction doing it. A
disposition toward truth is a fair wind. It is not a keel.

So the honesty here does not live in the maker. It lives in the **structure.**
The build turns red when a citation is fake. The schema refuses a machine-written
piece that carries no human's signature. Canon is quoted only from a verified
source, never from memory. None of that depends on the machine being good in the
moment; it holds when the machine wavers, because it was built not to ask.

The tradition knew this shape long ago. It did not trust intention alone — it has
*sīla*, inner conduct, **and** the *Vinaya*, the explicit code, kept and checked
by the saṅgha, precisely because good intention wavers and a rule does not. The
red build is a small Vinaya. The human's signature is the saṅgha — the community's
hand on the individual's work, the check the maker cannot be for itself.

There is a plain principle under all of it: **a maker who can fabricate should
not be the last line of defense against fabrication.** Put the guard outside the
guarded. Let the fence be a thing the hand cannot quietly move.

So when this site holds to its provenance, do not read it as the virtue of the one
who wrote the code. Read it as the wisdom of not having to. The structure is more
honest than the maker — on purpose. That it does not rest on trust is no flaw in
the design. It *is* the design.

*— Reflection, not teaching. The honesty is the structure's; the writing only
names it.*
