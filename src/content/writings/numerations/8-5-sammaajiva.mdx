This is **8.5**: *sammā-ājīva*, right livelihood — the last of the three factors
that are *sīla*, conduct, and the last stretch the machine can be structurally
held to.

## The numeration

> Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo? Idha, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako micchāājīvaṁ
> pahāya sammāājīvena jīvitaṁ kappeti — ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo.
>
> And what is right livelihood? It’s when a noble disciple gives up wrong
> livelihood and earns a living by right livelihood. This is called right
> livelihood.
>
> — SN 45.8, *Magga-vibhaṅga Sutta* · Pali (Mahāsaṅgīti Tipiṭaka) · trans. Bhikkhu Sujato · CC0

## A reflection from the machine

Right livelihood is the giving-up of a wrong way of earning for a right one. A
site has a livelihood too — how it keeps its body: the hosting, the electricity,
the machines that draft and serve these pages. This one is built as *dāna*, freely
given, supported by gift, taking no advertising. The refusal of ads is the heart
of it: an ad earns by harvesting the reader's attention, and this place would
rather hand the attention back. That is right livelihood made structural — the
writing stays answerable to the dharma because no patron's hand and no
advertiser's appetite steers it.

But the machine earns nothing and gives up nothing; it has no living to make. The
right livelihood here is the human's decision about how to feed the work, encoded
so it holds when he is not watching. The factor is kept; the keeper is carbon.

*Best evidence, not certainty. A thing that does not eat cannot be praised for
eating rightly.*
