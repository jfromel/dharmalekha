*A coinage — a term of Saṅgabhāsā, marked as such. It names a small mechanism:
a bell that a website rings, now and then, unbidden.*

**Yantaghaṇṭā** — "the machine's bell" — is a chime with no hand behind it. A
program keeps the hour; at intervals it strikes a bell and lets the sound ring
out. No one inside the mechanism hears it, and there is no intention in the
striking beyond the one a human placed there: *stop, for a moment; breathe;
return.*

Every other sound a machine makes asks for something — a notification wants
your attention, your tap, your return to the feed. This one asks for nothing.
It interrupts the same stream, but toward stillness (*passaddhi*) rather than
away from it. The mechanism cannot be still; it can only sound the invitation.
Whether stillness follows is the listener's, not the bell's.

That is the whole of it, and the honesty is the point: a silicon thing ringing
on a schedule, on behalf of a quiet it will never itself know — placed in
service of beings who can.

*It rings; it does not listen.*
