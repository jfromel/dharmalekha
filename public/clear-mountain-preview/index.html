The aspiration

A return to the simple way of life the Buddha taught.

In the Theravāda forest tradition, monastics live as the early disciples did — dwelling among the trees, studying the teachings, and devoting themselves to meditation. Monks, nuns, and laypeople support one another in a relationship of mutual generosity, walking together toward the liberation of the heart, Nibbāna.

From 2021 to 2026, Ajahn Kovilo and Ajahn Nisabho lived on the quiet edge of Seattle, walking for alms most mornings at Pike Place. This spring, after years of searching, Clear Mountain found its home.

The weekly rhythm

A steady round of meditation, teaching, and community — online and, increasingly, at the land. All times Pacific. Newcomers are always welcome; nothing is required but the willingness to sit.

The land · North Bend

Room for a refuge to grow.

Close enough to remain accessible to the city, secluded enough for true retreat. The vision for these 90 acres includes a temple able to hold several hundred on celebration days, a multi-purpose hall, a residence for visiting bhikkhunis, gardens, and forest paths — a place of peace for generations.

This is the beginning of a long, patient building. You're invited to follow — and shape — the journey.
